# Houston rain and storm impacts

Verified October 29, 2026. Displayed on both language versions of the home page. Values live in `src/data/houston-facts.ts`; every fact links to its source beside the claim.

| Fact | Scope and source |
| --- | --- |
| 51.84 inches a year | Houston Intercontinental Airport (IAH), 1991–2020 climate normals; not a metro-wide measurement or a forecast. [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/hgx/climate_iah_normals_summary). |
| 16.07 inches on August 27, 2017 | One calendar day at IAH during Harvey. About 31% is calculated as 16.07 / 51.84 × 100. No claim that this is a current all-time record. [NWS](https://www.weather.gov/hgx/climate_iah_normals_summary). |
| About $25,000 from one inch of water inside a home | Published warning, not a quote or a per-inch damage formula. Actual damage varies. [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/podcast/who-should-have-flood-insurance.html). |
| Estimated $125 billion in Harvey damage | Historical U.S. storm total, including wind and flooding, stated in 2017 dollars. Not Houston-only or a forecast of savings from a drainage system. [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/oklahoma-texas-water-science-center/science/post-hurricane-harvey-assessment), [NOAA's January 2018 report](https://www.noaa.gov/news/2017-was-3rd-warmest-year-on-record-for-us), [NOAA's 2017-dollar context](https://www.climate.gov/disasters-2017). Spanish renders “billion” as “mil millones,” not “billones.” |

The twelve monthly rainfall figures sum to the annual normal. The chart has a keyboard-accessible table; the visual bars are decorative duplicates. It does not estimate a home's future flood probability or financial losses. The next step links to the existing roof-runoff calculator.
