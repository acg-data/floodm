Heavy rain in Houston can turn a yard into a pond in under an hour. Most of the time the water drains away and life goes on. Sometimes it creeps toward the house, the garage or the back door. Here’s a calm, practical plan for both.

## During the storm: stay safe

**Never walk or drive through floodwater.** Moving water is stronger than it looks, and you can’t see what’s under it: open drains, washed-out pavement, debris or downed power lines. A few inches of moving water can knock a person off their feet, and a little more can float a car. The rule is simple: **Turn Around, Don’t Drown.**

**Stay out of standing water near electricity.** Keep away from flooded areas with outlets, extension cords, pool equipment or outdoor panels.

**Don’t try to dig a trench or move water in the storm.** It’s rarely effective and puts you outside in lightning and moving water.

**Watch official alerts.** Follow local emergency managers and the National Weather Service. In Harris County, the Harris County Flood Control District’s flood warning system shows rainfall and bayou levels in real time.

**Document how the water moves — from safely indoors.** Photos and short videos taken through a window are incredibly useful later. Capture where water enters the yard, where it collects, how deep it gets and which way it flows. That footage often tells us more than any after-the-fact description.

## If water is threatening your home

If water is approaching doors, the garage or the foundation and you need help, call our **24/7 emergency line at [(281) 849-1082](tel:+12818491082)**. We’ll talk through what you’re seeing and what can be done safely. Our [emergency page](/emergency/) lists what to have ready when you call.

If you’re in danger, or water is entering your home from a bayou, street or reservoir, call 911 and follow evacuation instructions. Yard drainage can’t hold back rising floodwater.

## Right after the storm

Once the rain has stopped and it’s safe to go outside:

- **Document everything for insurance.** Take photos and video of any water damage, high-water marks, damaged property and debris before you clean up. Keep receipts for anything you buy.
- **Call your insurer early** if water reached the house. Remember that flood damage and other water damage are often covered differently.
- **Check around the foundation.** Look for soil washed away from the slab, new gaps, exposed concrete, or erosion under walks and patios.
- **Clear grates and inlets.** Remove leaves, mulch and debris from catch basins, channel drains, pop-up emitters and the storm inlet in front of your house — only when the water has gone down and it’s safe.
- **Check your sump pump.** Make sure it’s running, cycling off when the basin is empty and discharging where it should. Listen for unusual noise.
- **Look at the driveway culvert** if you live on a street with open ditches. Debris often collects at the ends after a storm.
- **Note how long water stays.** Standing water that’s still there a day later is a sign the yard needs a better path out.

## In the weeks after

Floods reveal how your property really drains. Before memories fade:

- **Mark the problem areas** with flags or photos while they’re still muddy.
- **Write down where the water came from** — the roof, the street, a neighbor’s lot, or the ground itself.
- **Keep your videos.** They’re the best record you’ll have of how the yard behaves in a real storm.
- **Watch for slow drains.** Lines that took on silt during the storm may need cleaning or hydro jetting.

Then fix the cause, not just the symptom. A new drain dug in a hurry after a storm often ends up undersized or without a proper outlet. A plan designed around how water actually moves on your lot is what holds up in the next one.
