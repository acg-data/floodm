Drainage contractors tend to recommend whatever they install most. The better approach is to start from the water: where it collects, how long it stays, where it’s coming from and where it can go. This guide walks through the common Houston yard problems and the fixes that usually solve them.

## Start with three questions

**1. Where does the water sit?** Against the foundation, in the middle of the lawn, along a side yard, on a patio or at the end of a downspout? The location tells you a lot about the source.

**2. How long does it stay?** Water that drains within an hour or two after a heavy storm is normal in Houston clay. Water that’s still there the next day, or turf that stays spongy for most of the week, needs a fix.

**3. Is the low spot lower than the street or curb?** This is the question that decides between gravity and a pump. If water can flow downhill from the problem area to the street or another legal outlet, gravity drainage works. If it can’t, it needs to be lifted.

Take a short video of the yard during or right after a heavy rain. It answers all three questions better than any description.

## Match the problem to the fix

| What you’re seeing | Usual fix | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water standing in the lawn a day or more after rain | [French drain](/french-drain-installation-houston/) | Collects surface and subsurface water and carries it to an outlet |
| The low spot is lower than the street or curb | [Sump pump system](/sump-pump-installation-houston/) | Gravity can’t move water uphill; a pump can |
| The yard slopes toward the house | [Grading and runoff correction](/grading-drainage-houston/) | Reshaping the ground sends water away before it reaches the slab |
| Water pools at the end of every downspout | [Downspout lines and catch basins](/downspout-catch-basin-houston/) | Roof water gets piped underground to the curb or an emitter |
| Patios, driveways or pool decks hold water | [Channel and surface drains](/channel-drain-houston/) | A grate set into the hardscape catches water where it lands |
| Runoff cuts a path through beds or across the yard | [Dry creek bed or rock swale](/dry-creek-bed-houston/) | Gives the flow a defined, attractive channel |
| Water backs up at the driveway on an open-ditch street | [Culvert cleaning](/culvert-cleaning-houston/) or [replacement](/culvert-installation-houston/) | A silted, crushed or mis-set pipe blocks the ditch |
| An existing drain used to work but now floods | [Drain cleaning](/drain-cleaning-services-houston/) or [hydro jetting](/hydro-jetting-services-houston/) | Silt, leaves and roots reduce a line’s capacity over time |

## French drains

A French drain is a gravel-filled trench around a perforated pipe, usually wrapped in filter fabric. Water from the surface and the surrounding soil enters the trench and the pipe carries it away. It’s the go-to fix for lawns and side yards that stay wet, and for water coming from a neighbor’s lot. In Houston’s slow-draining clay, it has to be designed with an outlet and enough slope, not just buried and hoped for.

## Sump pump systems

When the wet area sits lower than every possible outlet, no amount of gravel will help. A yard sump system collects water from drains into a buried basin, and a pump pushes it out through a solid discharge line to the curb. It’s common in backyards behind raised homes and on lots lower than the street.

## Grading and runoff

Sometimes the ground itself is the problem. Beds and mulch build up against the house, patios settle, and builder grading changes as sod sinks. Reshaping the yard so it falls away from the foundation is often the most effective and longest-lasting fix, and it makes every other drain work better.

## Downspouts and catch basins

A 2,400-square-foot roof sheds thousands of gallons in a heavy Houston storm, and all of it leaves through a few downspouts. If those downspouts dump at the slab, no other drain will keep up. Burying them in solid pipe to a curb outlet or pop-up emitter is one of the highest-value upgrades a Houston home can make. Catch basins collect water in low corners and feed the same lines.

## Channel and surface drains

Hardscape doesn’t absorb anything. Channel drains (long grated trenches) and square surface drains catch water on patios, pool decks and driveways and route it into the underground system.

## Dry creek beds and rock swales

Where runoff naturally crosses the yard, a shallow channel lined with river rock carries it in a defined path. It works as drainage and reads as landscaping, which makes it popular on wooded and heavily planted lots.

## Most yards need more than one

It’s rare for a Houston yard to have a single problem. A typical plan might pipe the downspouts, regrade a bed that slopes toward the house and add a French drain along a soggy side yard, all tied to one outlet. Designing the pieces together is what keeps them from fighting each other.
